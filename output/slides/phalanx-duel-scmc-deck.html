Learn · 01 — Where this came from

It started because it was raining.

July 2022, Camp Tesomas. I was an adult leader on my son's troop trip when a storm pushed everyone into their tents. No phone service, no game night, and I'd left my Magic: The Gathering deck at home. So I grabbed a notebook and a plain deck of cards from the camp trading post and started sketching.

A week of tent-flap playtesting worked out the damage math, gave each suit a job, and landed on a four‑column battlefield. The rules in that notebook are still the rules you're about to learn.

Learn · 02 — Setup

Two decks. Four columns. 20 life points.

Each player shuffles a standard 52‑card deck and draws 12. Cards go face up into four columns, two ranks deep: a front rank that attacks and can be attacked, a back rank held in reserve. Columns are numbered from your own left — you sit facing your opponent, so your column 1 lines up across from their column 4.

Learn · 03 — On your turn

Attack once, or pass.

  1. Choose. Pick one of your front cards and any enemy column.
  2. Resolve. Damage travels through the column: front, back, then life points.
  3. Rebuild. The defender may fill empty slots — only in the attacked column.
  4. Switch. The defender draws to four, then becomes the attacker.

Learn · 05 — The four suits

Timing rules, not superpowers.

Each suit fires only at the boundary it names.

♦ Diamond · Shield

When a destroyed Diamond's carryover is headed to another card, subtract the Diamond's own value first.

♥ Heart · Last guard

If the final card destroyed before life points is a Heart, subtract its value before that damage lands.

♣ Club · Breakthrough

A Club attacker doubles carryover once, the first time it crosses from one card to another.

♠ Spade · Finishing blow

A Spade attacker doubles the carryover the instant it crosses into life points.

Learn · 06 — Answering and winning

Rebuild the column. Reach zero.

  • ▪After an attack, the defender may reinforce only the column that was hit, then draws back to four cards and becomes the attacker.
  • ▪A card that survived a hit returns to full value next turn — damage doesn't carry between turns.
  • ▪Reach 0 life points and you win. Passing is legal, but three passes in a row, or five in a game, forfeits the duel.
  • ▪Running out of cards to draw is not a loss. There is no reshuffle.

Play · Now

Grab a deck

Now play a hand.

Pair up. Each of you needs a standard 52‑card deck — I brought extras if you didn't. Shuffle, draw twelve, build your formation, and play until someone breaks through. About ten minutes. If a rule is disputed, wave me down.

Setup → a turn → resolve an attack → the four suits → reinforce → win. That's the whole game.

Code · 05 — Why it's built this way

What you verified by hand is what the server verifies every turn.

  • ▪No dice, no shuffle mid‑match. Same inputs, same outcome, every time.
  • ▪Every match keeps a replay log — independently verifiable, not just trusted.
  • ▪AI–assisted, not AI–authored: a human architect sets the contract, agents implement inside it, and verification gates — not the model's own claims — decide what merges.
